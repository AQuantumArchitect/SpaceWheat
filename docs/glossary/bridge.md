A **bridge** is the first structure the [Bath](bath.md) cannot reach: one
fermion split into two end-modes, anchored to an atom pair on each of two
shores. The stored bit is the pair's **joint parity** — readable at neither
end alone, held between the places, in neither. It is a two-by-two register
that belongs to no biome's cloud; the world's machinery never sees it.

Its protection is arithmetic, not armor: the Bath drinks only where it has a
local handle, and a nonlocal parity offers none. A bridge fades at the
**product** of its two shores' wet rates — so one anchor in closed country
(home — the [enclave](enclave.md) that stayed closed) makes the span
immortal. The island the door never opened is the strongest foundation in
the open world.

A bridge accepts no gates. **Braiding** its end-modes is the only writing it
understands, and braiding speaks exactly two letters — S on one shore, √X on
the other — an honest alphabet the scholars call Clifford. The letters do
not commute; the braid *word* is the program.

Reading is **fusion**: the parity is Born-sampled, paid in surprisal across
both shores at once, and the bridge is spent — because localizing the answer
hands the Bath the address. Looking closes it
([measurement](measurement.md)'s oldest rule, on its newest instrument).
Played as acts 5–7 of "What Connects" (`docs/CONNECT_CAMPAIGN.md`);
machinery in `Core/QuantumSubstrate/BridgeRegister.gd`.
