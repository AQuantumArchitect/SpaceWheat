An **invariant** is a quantity that unitary evolution cannot touch. The enclave deforms
every state smoothly and reversibly — populations slosh, coherences rotate, nothing is
ever lost — and an invariant is what all that motion slides right off of. It does not
drift. It does not decay. If it changes at all, it *jumps*, and a jump needs an act the
enclave counts as irreversible.

Measurement is the enclave's *thermodynamic* irreversibility. Invariants are its
*geometric* one — and the topology campaign (`docs/TOPOLOGY_CAMPAIGN.md`, "What
Survives") teaches four of them, one per chapter:

1. **The solid angle a closed loop encloses** — the [berry](berry.md). Two roads from
   the same door to the same door carry different sky; only the shape of the walk is
   kept. *(Arc: `loop_remembers`, act 1.)*
2. **The spectrum of ρ** — the eigenvalues of a biome's density matrix. Every unitary
   conserves them; the surface moves, the depths do not. Exactly one hand reaches the
   depths: [measurement](measurement.md). *(Arc: `pond_depths` / `pond_breathes`,
   act 2. The compass 🧭 on the Graph frame reads the dominant depth and its gap.)*
3. **A chain's winding** — how many weak-strong walls stand between an edge and the
   world, an integer, and integers cannot change smoothly. The Lamplighters' chain at
   Lanternfall keeps its bridge lit on exactly this. *(Arc: `chain_ends` /
   `chain_flipped`, act 3.)*
4. **The order of a braid** — non-commuting operations form words, not sets;
   H-then-CNOT and CNOT-then-H are different worlds. Order survives when every state
   it touched has been rotated away. *(Arc: `braid_order` / `braid_word`, act 4.)*

The campaign's thesis, one line: **the [enclave](enclave.md) forbids decay; it cannot
forbid shape.** In a world where nothing rusts, the only things worth farming are the
things that could never rust anyway.
