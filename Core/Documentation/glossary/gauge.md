Every plot keeps a **local phase convention** — a private zero for its clock
face. **Gauge freedom** is the fact that you may turn any plot's convention by
any angle χ, rewriting every fence that touches it
(A_ij → A_ij + χ_i − χ_j), and *no prediction anywhere changes*. Half the
numbers on your screen are yours, not the world's.

The Operator's 🧭 compass mode hands you the turn as a verb — and it is
**free**, deliberately: changing bookkeeping costs nothing because nothing
happens. The lesson is in what refuses to move. Walk any closed circuit of
fences and take the product: every χ enters twice with opposite signs, so the
loop total never hears the turning. What a turn can change was never physics;
what no turn can change — the [wilson](wilson.md) loops — is what the world
actually keeps.

Nothing here persists in a save, and that too is the lesson: reload, and your
conventions re-seed from the Hamiltonian while every loop product comes back
exactly — the invariant content never depended on your bookkeeping. Played as
"What Turns" (`docs/GAUGE_CAMPAIGN.md`); machinery in
`Core/QuantumSubstrate/GaugeField.gd`.
